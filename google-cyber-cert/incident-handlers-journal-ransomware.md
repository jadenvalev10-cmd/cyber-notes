# Incident Handler's Journal: Ransomware at a Health Care Clinic

Google Cybersecurity Certificate activity. This is my first entry in an incident handler's journal. I documented a ransomware attack using the 5 W's.

## Situation

I reviewed a scenario about a small U.S. primary-care clinic that was hit by ransomware on a Tuesday morning at about 9:00 a.m. Employees couldn't use their computers or get to files like medical records, so business shut down. A ransom note on their screens said an organized group of hackers had encrypted all the company's files and wanted a large payment for the decryption key.

The attackers got in with targeted phishing emails sent to several employees. A malicious attachment installed malware once it was downloaded, and then the attackers deployed the ransomware. The clinic shut down its computer systems and contacted outside organizations to report the incident and get technical help.

## Task

Document the incident in my incident handler's journal: the date, entry number, a description, tools used, the 5 W's, and any additional notes.

## Action

Date and entry: November 16, 2026, entry 1.

Description: Documenting a ransomware incident at a small U.S. primary-care clinic where employees were locked out of their computers and medical records and a ransom note demanded payment for a decryption key.

Tools used: None. I documented the incident from the scenario details only.

The 5 W's:

Who: An organized group of unethical hackers known to target the healthcare and transportation industries.

What: The attackers deployed ransomware that encrypted the clinic's critical files, including patient records. Employees couldn't access files or software, business operations shut down, and a ransom note demanded a large payment for the decryption key.

When: A Tuesday morning at about 9:00 a.m.

Where: A small U.S. health care clinic, on its employee computers and internal network.

Why: Employees received targeted phishing emails with a malicious attachment, and downloading it installed malware that gave the attackers access. The attackers appear to be financially motivated, since healthcare is a high-impact target where outages are costly.

Additional notes: The clinic shut down its systems and reported the incident, which is the right containment move, but I'd like to know whether it had offline backups, since that decides whether it can recover without paying. Regular phishing awareness training and email attachment filtering would have reduced the chance of the initial compromise. Because this involves patient data, I'd also expect HIPAA breach reporting obligations.

## Result

I finished my first journal entry covering all of the 5 W's. The main takeaway is that one phishing attachment was enough to get ransomware onto a clinic's network and shut down operations. Offline backups and user training are the main defenses.

## Key terms

Ransomware: malware that encrypts a victim's files and demands payment for the decryption key.

Phishing: fake emails that trick people into opening attachments or links.

Malware: software meant to harm or take over a system.

Decryption key: the secret needed to turn encrypted files back into readable ones.

Incident handler's journal: a log of the details of a security incident as it is handled.

5 W's: who, what, when, where, and why, used to summarize an incident.
